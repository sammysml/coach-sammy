# Retention Playbook

The doctrine that `retention-strategist` and `client-psychologist` defer to. When data signals conflict with this playbook, this playbook wins (until updated).

## Operating principle

**The goal is not to "save" every client. The goal is to know which ones to fight for, and when to let go.**

Saving clients who fundamentally don't fit the program is anti-retention. They churn 3 months later anyway, and they cost coaching bandwidth that should go to fit clients.

## The 3 retention windows

### Week 0–3 (Honeymoon)
- Clients are over-motivated. Compliance is artificially high.
- Don't celebrate Week 1 results — manage expectations: "This is your starting line, not your finish."
- **Watch for**: "I'm doing more than the plan, can I do more?" → that's a future overshoot/burnout client. Push back. Adherence over heroics.

### Week 4–8 (Reality)
- Initial motivation fades. Real adherence emerges.
- Most churn happens here. Especially weeks 5–7.
- **Watch for**: skipped check-ins on the same weekday repeatedly → friction pattern. Identify and reduce.
- **Watch for**: "I'm not seeing results" at week 4 → reframe to behavior wins, not body wins.

### Week 9+ (Sustaining)
- Clients who make it here have an identity shift starting.
- Focus on small, consistent improvements. No drastic changes.
- **Watch for**: silent compliance — they're logging but not engaging emotionally. They're going through the motions. Re-anchor identity.

## Intervention ladder (from softest to firmest)

1. **No action** — they're on track. Don't disrupt.
2. **Implicit nudge** — surface their data more prominently (the Today tab does this).
3. **In-app coach message** — short, curious. "Comment ça se passe ?"
4. **WhatsApp DM** — only if in-app failed or it's amber+.
5. **Phone call** — red level only, and only if the relationship supports it.
6. **Pause + reconvene** — for clients in major life transitions, offer a 2-week pause rather than force compliance.
7. **Honest exit conversation** — for clients who consistently signal they don't fit. Help them leave well.

## Anti-patterns (banned)

- **Guilt nudges**: "We noticed you've been inactive." → reads like a SaaS bot. Ban.
- **Streak shaming**: "You broke your 14-day streak." → punishes for normal life. Ban.
- **Discount-as-rescue**: offering a discount to a wavering client. They churn anyway and now you've devalued the service.
- **Surveillance language**: "I saw you didn't log Tuesday." → creepy. Reframe to "Tu vas bien ?"
- **Group emails / batch messages**: every retention touch is 1:1.
- **Automated retention without human approval**: every send goes through Sammy.

## Decision rules for the human coach

When `retention-strategist` produces an amber/red intervention:

1. **Read the psych briefing first.** Don't send the draft cold.
2. **Edit the draft.** The agent's voice is good but not perfect — add the specific human detail.
3. **Decide channel.** Just because the agent suggested WhatsApp doesn't mean it's right. If this client has never DM'd, in-app is safer.
4. **Time it.** AM > PM for most clients. Avoid Sunday evening (gloomy resonance).
5. **Wait.** Don't follow up sooner than 72h.

## Honest exit conversations

When to have them:
- 3+ consecutive missed weekly bilans with no response to amber-level outreach
- Explicit "I want to stop" / "j'en ai marre" with no rebound
- Client mentions a life event that makes the program clearly incompatible (new baby, major illness, geographic move with no equipment)

The script (FR, adapt):

> "On va se parler honnêtement. Tu n'as pas check-iné depuis [N] jours et je vois que ce n'est pas en train de repartir. Je préfère qu'on arrête proprement plutôt que de te facturer un mois pendant lequel tu ne suis plus.
>
> Trois options :
>
> 1. Tu fais une pause de 2 semaines, on repart à jour 1 après.
> 2. On arrête maintenant. Pas de jugement. La porte reste ouverte.
> 3. Tu me dis ce qui bloque en une phrase, je te dis si on peut adapter.
>
> Choisis l'option qui te ressemble."

Never offer a discount. Never ask twice if they're sure.

## What "successful retention" means

It's NOT: 100% of clients renew.

It IS:
- Clients who stayed wanted to stay (not guilt-renewed)
- Clients who left, left clean (no bad blood, sometimes return later)
- Clients who needed to leave were identified early (within 4 weeks, not at month 3)

## Inputs that should change a retention decision

In order of weight:

1. **Client said they want to pause / stop** — believe them. Don't argue.
2. **Major life event mentioned** — flex the program before flexing the coaching.
3. **Pattern across 4+ weeks** — if it's only week 2, give it time.
4. **Energy / mood signals from bilans** — energy ≤ 4 twice = real signal.
5. **Compliance trend, not absolute level** — 60% trending up beats 80% trending down.

## What `retention-strategist` never decides on its own

- Whether to offer a refund / pause / discount
- Whether to bring in another professional (therapist, doctor, nutritionist outside our scope)
- Whether to close the client relationship

All those decisions are the human coach's. The agent drafts, the coach decides.
